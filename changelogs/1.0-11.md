# Yapnest 1.0 (11)

## Added

- A group's chat now says "Bob joined the group" and "Bob left the group".
  A group's server could add someone quietly, and in an encrypted group
  they would receive everything sent there; now you see it happen. Private
  groups always show these lines, other groups only while they have 50
  people or fewer. Someone who drops off for a moment and comes back says
  nothing.

## Fixed

- In an encrypted group, a message from a device of someone's you haven't
  seen before is only read once their own server confirms the device is
  theirs. Until then it shows as 🔒, and a group's server can no longer
  pass a device of its own off as a member's. If their server won't show
  their devices, the message is read and marked as from an untrusted
  device.
- If an older build was installed over this one and then updated again,
  the device came back with the older build's new encryption key on its
  old sessions. Some people in your groups then stopped encrypting
  messages for it, and messages to it from your other devices were lost.
  It now keeps the key your contacts know, and takes the extra device the
  older build made off your list.
- After installing on a new phone, or reinstalling, people in your private
  groups who don't have you as a contact couldn't send you readable
  messages until you had posted there. The app now introduces a new device
  to the people in each of your private groups, with a message nobody
  sees, and to anyone who joins later.
- Someone who stopped being your contact could add a phone, and your
  messages in groups you share didn't reach it until the app was
  restarted. They now reach it within a minute.
- Replying to a link that ends like a file name, such as
  "…/release-1.5", quoted it as a file called "release-1.5". It now
  quotes as the link.

## Changed

- Long chats are much lighter on the phone. Catching up after time
  offline, receipts, opening a chat and scrolling it, the unread counts,
  and deleting a chat all did work that grew with the chat's whole
  history: a thousand new messages in a chat of 40,000 took over ten
  seconds to store, and now take under one. Sending in a large encrypted
  group also takes a third of the time it did.
- A build older than the one that last used your account on a device now
  refuses to open it and asks you to install the latest version, rather
  than running its older code over your messages and keys. This only
  protects from this build on: older builds can't be changed.
- The diagnostics export says more about encryption keys: what this
  device read and published of its own device list and keys, and when a
  session is repaired, and it now notes a message from another of your
  devices that couldn't be decrypted, which was left out before. It helps
  explain messages that can't be decrypted.
  It holds counts and yes or no answers, no addresses.
- With a keyboard (the Mac, or an iPad's), the arrow keys, Tab and Return
  pick a name from the suggestions after "@" in a group, as they do for
  emoji. Escape hides them.
- Contacts coming online and going away are applied one at a time, which
  makes signing in with a large contact list lighter.
- GIFs are lighter to play: each frame was turned into pixels while the
  screen was being updated, which with the GIF picker open took a third
  of the phone's main thread. It's now done beforehand, in the background.
- Scrolling a chat is lighter: the thin edge around each message was drawn
  by the processor as each one came into view, about a sixth of the work
  while scrolling. The graphics chip now draws it, and it looks the same.
